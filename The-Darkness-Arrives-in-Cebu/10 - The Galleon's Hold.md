# 10 - The Galleon's Hold

## Lyrics

[Spoken Intro]
October 1565. Ten crates for the saints in Mexico —
the manifest says ten; the stevedores count eleven.

[Verse 1]
Lamp and windlass; the carvings go last, wrapped in sail-cloth —
the lad counts eleven where the manifest lied.

[Chorus]
Down in the hold, in a dead man's sail-cloth,
a crate has the sound of a child's soft chin —
tick, tick, warm to the hand though the sea runs cold.
A hull keeps what a hull is told to hold.

[Verse 2]
The cargo master heard the breathing, the tap of a small flat palm —
"Rain," he said, and hurried along.

[Bridge]
The carpenter's boy found the box but did not open it:
it sang in his dead mother's key.

[Outro]
[Spoken — the captain, at dawn]
My first mate keeps asking which crate we call the passenger.
I have told him we are all the passenger, at sea —
the first lie I have told that I did not believe.
