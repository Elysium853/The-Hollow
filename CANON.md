# The Hollow — Canon

*The single source of truth for names, timeline, terminology, and the arithmetic every track must obey. The README carries the cast and story summary; `.github/MEMORY.md` carries the working notes; this file is the canonical layer both of them reference. `scripts/check-parity.sh` enforces the automated parts (§ Arithmetic lock → "Enforced by automation").*

## The one-line canon

A hunger older than light — buried beneath the world, starving on fullness — was refused by godhood's own daughters and carried across the Pacific inside four family bloodlines, until one ordinary weekend in 2025, four Filipino women built one fire in a fold of Kentucky woods and the count began to close. The saga is the count: descriptive until the answer, arithmetic in the last movement, and resolved not by war but by fullness.

## Timeline (album-by-anchor)

The README, the playlist tables, and the cast page present the saga in **release order** (opening chapter → main line → prequel trilogy → origin); the anchors below follow the story's own chronology.

### Before Creation — *Dawn of the Void*
- **Tohu**, the last of the great ones, made of space rather than light, buried beneath the world, starving on fullness, becomes **the Hollow**.
- **The Cherubim** — the four-faced chariot (lion, ox, man, eagle) — the origin of every count in four the Hollow is later caged by.
- **Bugan and Obban**, daughters of Lumawig, refuse godhood in the rice field and the childbirth hut — the refusal that seeds the whole count.
- **Bathala the Keeper** — the earth-name of the Keeper of the Door, **the seal's fifth point**, appointed at the first dawn; the anti-appetite who starves on wanting; the doctrine *"Not by blade. By fullness."*
- **Dalisay** frames the whole scroll as a vision (her own arc is Album 5).

### April 1521 — Mactan framing (*prologue of the Cebu album*)
- April 27, 1521: Magellan dies at Mactan; the island has learned the white men can bleed.
- The farewell feast (May 1) is a trap — "twenty-nine iron men died at the waterline."
- The **child in the tindalo box** is left with the queen: Nara — the hidden one whose name will be stolen. The hollow ("the child") is fed through **forty-four years**.

### 1565 — Cebu: the sandugo and the tornaviaje
- Legazpi's fleet (1564–65, out of Puerto de la Navidad): five ships, 380 men, five Augustinians — among them **Fray Andrés de Urdaneta**, fifty-six, who carries the Pacific in his head.
- **The sandugo at Bohol** ("one blood, poured in Bohol") — the first binding; the blood compact of alliance.
- April 27, 1565: Cebu burned to the waterline; the tindalo box found whole in the ash, warm from inside.
- The keeper gives the hollow its "travel papers" to Urdaneta.
- **July 1565**: Datu Tupas kneels — "the fourth binding was being sealed with a king's word"; the old women **count four**; **the fourth name goes into the water at Cebu**, and the hollow's count becomes possible.
- **October–November 1565 — the tornaviaje**: the *San Pedro* sails east on "the return, the road that ran against the sun." The manifest lists ten; the stevedores count eleven.

### 1565–1841 — The Eleventh Figure: the galleon road
- **November 8, 1565 — Acapulco**: ten on the manifest, **eleven in the store-room of the new church**; the count of the road begins.
- The crate travels the mule road to Mexico City and the mission roads of New Spain — the **galleon road** the hollow rides like a song learning a singer.
- **1811 — the church**: the congregation of the two rivers builds the church over the red-creek ground; the warm child is hung above the table, smiling and counting, until the fire of dry October.
- **1841 — the hollow takes Kentucky**: the count of four hundred years of roads ends in the cup-shaped green valley — "the dark had been waiting politely since 1841."

### 1841–2025 — the long patience
- The burned-church ground, the county roads, the filling station, the man with the gray face who sells ice and never looks at the road.
- Four grandmother bloodlines carry the count across the generations so that one ordinary Friday the truck can be packed.

### Summer 2025 — *The Red Hollow of Kentucky* ("the present")
- **Lina** (46, b. 1979), **Marisol**, **Tess**, and **Jo** (47, b. 1978) — four tents, **one fire**; four friends, one red cooler, the thing that counts what gathers.
- **Ulan** (20, b. ~2005), Lina's daughter, is away at college — "Mom, you look tired."
- The four leave in one truck with four bodies and uncertain souls; the Hollow takes the fourth name it was given ("the fourth name is not hers").

### The Amuyao Covenant (Album 2)
- The four return to the mountains of Luzon; the covenant is sealed naked and blood-marked in the circle of trees on **Mt. Amuyao** — one hive in four bodies.
- The followers collect on the road south, doubling as they go: four, then eight, then sixteen.

### The Unholy Blood (Album 3)
- The temple beneath Manila's caves; the gathered thousands on the plains; **the star wants its center** — the geometry the four are building.

### The Permanent Season (Album 4)
- Lumawig's barrier is **a denial, not a wall**; it holds the hungry in place for **seven years, or seven minutes** — time in the caves moves like blood through water.
- Jo finds the crack: *"The barrier holds the hungry. But it does not hold the fed."* The four feed on newborn blood; the sleepwalking mothers of Manila walk into the cave mouths.
- The survivors of the old guard carry the count up into the mountains, toward a girl already dreaming in the blood of Bugan and Obban: **Dalisay**.

### The Solitary Path (Album 5)
- **Dalisay, forty-six, then forty-seven**: thirty years of refusal above Batad — the refusal began at sixteen, the night Lumawig whispered the secret words and she said no.
- The stone house in the terraces, Lumawig's bamboo library (spilled from the mountain in the landslide), the rusted blade that cut her palm; her mother, who died when she was twenty; her father, who beat her for the second sight.
- The four in dreams wear Lina's memory, Marisol's voice, Tess's curiosity, and the emptiness that answers to Nara.

### 2057 — *The Hollow Destroyed* (Album 6, "+32 years")
- **Ulan, fifty-two**, called by dreams to the stone house above Batad; her mother, **Lina, frozen at forty-six** ("You look younger than me").
- The Restoration: Dalisay steps into the star as **the fifth point** and completes it with wholeness rather than joining — *"What they consumed, you will restore. What they took, you will return. Not by blade. By fullness."*
- The Hollow starves on the wholeness it cannot digest — *"We are full. We are enough. We refuse you."* — and goes silent; the palace of bone and the Lower Choirs dissolve.
- Six months on: the wedding in Manila — **Lina and Marisol**, the love that waited **twenty years**, married by Dalisay with Lumawig's blade.

## The arithmetic lock

Every track obeys this; nothing may alter it, including lines that merely *feel* like counting.

1. **The count.** *"Ten, eleven, twelve, and one to come."* The grandmothers' count of the hollow — fixed in `Bloodlines/01 - The Grandmothers' Fire.md` and mechanically pinned by `check-parity.sh`. The sequence never changes and the "one to come" is never fulfilled within the saga: even after the Restoration the count holds and the door stays open.
2. **Four bloods → one fire becomes four.** Four bloodlines made the count possible — the busaw feast of the Visayas (Bathilang → Lina), the mangkukulam covenant of the Tagalog lowlands (→ Marisol), the babaylan who turned to the serpents of deep earth (→ Tess), and the anito tables of Bicol (→ Jo). The prologue's *"one fire that has not been lit yet"* is lit in Kentucky and **becomes four** — never five, never fewer.
3. **The four and the fifth are invariant.** The star has exactly **four points** (the four women; later the Empresses of Hunger) and wants exactly **one fifth** — its center: Dalisay, who fills it with wholeness. Bathala the Keeper held the same seat on the seal. No track may imply three or five points among the women, a sixth point, or a second fifth.
4. **Ages that pin the arithmetic** (the P0 lesson — stale age numbers are the one canon leak that has shipped): refusal began at **16**; Dalisay **46 → 47** in Album 5; Lina **46** at Red Hollow and frozen at **46**; Ulan **20 → 52** across the **thirty-two-year** gap; the hidden love waited **twenty years**; the "**seven years, or seven minutes**" of the binding.

### Enforced by automation (`scripts/check-parity.sh`, text layer)
- **Banned stale values** in lyric files: the words `thirty-six` and `forty-three`, and the phrase `fifteen years` in a love/waiting/hidden context (`of waiting`, `of your love`, `of hidden love`, `hidden`, `I loved in silence`) — the hidden love waited **twenty** years, so `Fifteen years I worked beside you` (the ward stint) stays legal. Any re-introduction of these re-opens the P0 leak and the script fails.
- The **count lock line** must stay in `Bloodlines/01 - The Grandmothers' Fire.md`.
- Every lyric file needs its `## Lyrics` section; no tracked text file may carry CR (non-LF) line endings.

### Known wrinkles (flagged, not yet enforced)
- `The-Hollow-Destroyed/13 - Lumawig's Blade.md` (outro) sings "Six points of the star / Six women made whole" — reconcile against the four-plus-fifth geometry before that track is ever touched for another reason. The Restoration prose's closing summary table (the story's `## The Six Points`, added 2026-08-28) names the same six — Lina, Marisol, Tess, Jo, Dalisay, Ulan — read as the four points plus the fifth (Dalisay, the center) plus Ulan, not six star-points; the wrinkle stands until that track is touched for another reason.

## Glossary of Filipino terms used in the lyrics

Each entry: the plain meaning, then how the saga uses it. Keep new tracks accurate to both.

### Contracts and crossings
- **sandugo** — the Visayan blood compact: two parties seal alliance by drinking wine mixed with each other's blood. *The saga:* the 1565 Bohol compact — "one blood, poured in Bohol" — the first of the four bindings; the count runs out from it.
- **tornaviaje** — the "return voyage": Urdaneta's 1565 eastward Pacific crossing that opened the Manila–Acapulco galleon road. *The saga:* "the road that ran against the sun" — the highway the hollow rides into the New World.

### Bloodlines (the four)
- **busaw** — in Cordillera lore, a devouring famine spirit; corruption-hunger, gluttony, the things that attend the unclean world. *The saga:* the Visayan feast-blood (Bathilang); "filth spirits, hunger spirits, hoarders of breath" — Lina's line.
- **mangkukulam** — a practitioner of *kulam*, Tagalog folk hex-sorcery. *The saga:* the Tagalog covenant-sorcerers who traded humanity for longevity, "shadow for shadow, name for name" — Marisol's line.
- **babaylan** — the pre-colonial Visayan shaman-priestess: healer, medium, keeper of the ritual. *The saga:* the one who turned from the light to the *dalag* serpents of deep earth and learned "the wrong weather, the plural *we*" — Tess's line.
- **anito** — ancestral and nature spirits of indigenous Filipino belief, honored at household tables. *The saga:* the Bicol tables that "feed the dead a thread of living red," keepers of the fourth name since 1565 — Jo's line.

### Priesthood and instruments
- **mumbaki** — the Ifugao ritual priests who chant the ancient epics and officiate the rites. *The saga:* Lumawig's priests — "the old men with chicken bones and rusted machetes"; Dalisay ends as "the last mumbaki."
- **kulintang** — the row of bossed gongs that makes up the south's gong-chime ensemble. *The saga:* one of the signature braided voices of the metal layer.
- **agung** — the large hanging bossed gong, the bass of the kulintang ensemble. *The saga:* the lowest voice of the island-metal braid.
- **gangsa** — the flat bronze gongs of the Cordillera (Ifugao/Kalinga) — the same highlands as Lumawig and the mumbaki. *The saga:* the mountain's own percussion.
- **kudyapi** — the two-stringed boat-lute of Mindanao, plucked like a song learning a singer. *The saga:* melodic voice of the pre-colonial layer.
- **dabakan** — the goblet drum of the kulintang ensemble, played with two sticks. *The saga:* the groove under the grandmothers.
- **subing** — the bamboo jaw harp (also *kubing*), played across the islands. *The saga:* the reed-voice in the braid, kin to the bamboo flutes.

### Also in the lyrics
- **dalag** — the mud-eels / eel-serpents of the deep earth; the babaylan's turn ("the dalag, the deep-earth serpents").
- **tindalo** — the close-grained hardwood of the box that carried the child: "a box of tindalo wood, dark as water, closed with silver nails."
- **anak ko** — Tagalog "my child / my daughter": Ulan's mother-song in `05 - Mother.md`.

## Rules for new tracks and contributors

1. Cite the timeline by anchor, not by album-table position: ages and dates come from this file (and the albums named next to them), never from memory.
2. Keep the count unbroken: ten, eleven, twelve, and one to come — never completed, never reordered, never made three, five, or six by any track that is not an explicit in-world miscount.
3. Keep the four and the fifth invariant: exactly four women/points/bloods, exactly one fifth (Dalisay at the center). If a lyric needs a number of people in a scene, count the speakers, not the star's points.
4. Never invent a new age for Lina, Marisol, Tess, Jo, Ulan, or Dalisay (see the arithmetic lock, rule 4).
5. Use the glossary for Filipino terms; if a new term is needed, add it here (and to the lyric) rather than improvising a meaning.
6. Every lyric file keeps its `## Lyrics` section and LF line endings; every change must leave `scripts/check-parity.sh` at exit 0.
