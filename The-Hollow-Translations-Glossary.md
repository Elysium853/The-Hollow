# The Hollow — Translations & Glossary

*English translations for every non-English word and phrase used in the prose, lyrics, and canon of the saga.*

> The Hollow is told in the voice of grandmothers — Filipino-diaspora women who sing the count across the widest water in the world. Its non-English vocabulary is drawn from **Filipino and indigenous Philippine languages** (Tagalog, Visayan/Cebuano, Ifugao, Bicol), **Spanish** (the colonial centuries that carried the islands' children — and one warm child in a tindalo box — across the Pacific), and **Hebrew/Latin** (the scripture of the void-before-light). This document translates and explains every one.

---

## Part I — The bloodlines and their covenants

| Word | Language | English / Explanation |
|------|----------|----------------------|
| **busaw** | Visayan (Cordillera lore) | A devouring famine spirit; corruption-hunger, gluttony, the unclean. *The saga:* the Visayan feast-blood of Bathilang — Lina's line |
| **mangkukulam** | Tagalog | A practitioner of *kulam* (folk hex-sorcery). *The saga:* the Tagalog covenant-sorcerers who traded humanity for longevity, "shadow for shadow, name for name" — Marisol's line |
| **babaylan** | Visayan | The pre-colonial shaman-priestess: healer, medium, keeper of ritual. *The saga:* the one who turned from the light to the *dalag* serpents of deep earth — Tess's line |
| **anito** | Tagalog / indigenous | Ancestral and nature spirits honored at household tables. *The saga:* the Bicol tables that "feed the dead a thread of living red," keepers of the fourth name since 1565 — Jo's line |
| **dalag** | Tagalog | The mud-eel / eel-serpent of the deep earth; the babaylan's turn ("the dalag, the deep-earth serpents") |

## Part II — Contracts, crossings, and the road

| Word | Language | English / Explanation |
|------|----------|----------------------|
| **sandugo** | Visayan | "One blood" — the blood compact: two parties seal alliance by drinking wine mixed with each other's blood. *The saga:* the 1565 Bohol compact — "one blood, poured in Bohol" — the first of the four bindings; the count runs out from it |
| **tornaviaje** | Spanish | "The return voyage" — Urdaneta's 1565 eastward Pacific crossing that opened the Manila–Acapulco galleon road. *The saga:* "the road that ran against the sun" — the highway the hollow rides into the New World |
| **tindalo** | Tagalog | The close-grained hardwood of the box that carried the child: "a box of tindalo wood, dark as water, closed with silver nails" |
| **carabao** | Spanish (via Tagalog) | The water buffalo — a patient beast of burden of the islands |

## Part III — Priesthood, music, and the old instruments

| Word | Language | English / Explanation |
|------|----------|----------------------|
| **mumbaki** | Ifugao | The ritual priests who chant the ancient epics and officiate the rites. *The saga:* Lumawig's priests — "the old men with chicken bones and rusted machetes"; Dalisay ends as "the last mumbaki" |
| **datu** | Various (Visayan/Malay) | A chief or chieftain — a local ruler (Rajah Humabon, Datu Tupas, Lapu-Lapu) |
| **kulintang** | Mindanao | The row of bossed bronze gongs that forms a gong-chime ensemble — one of the signature braided voices of the metal layer |
| **agung** | Mindanao | The large hanging bossed gong, the bass of the kulintang ensemble — the lowest voice of the island-metal braid |
| **gangsa** | Cordillera (Ifugao/Kalinga) | The flat bronze gongs of the highlands — the mountain's own percussion |
| **kudyapi** | Mindanao | The two-stringed boat-lute, plucked "like a song learning a singer" — the melodic voice of the pre-colonial layer |
| **dabakan** | Mindanao | The goblet drum of the kulintang ensemble, played with two sticks — the groove under the grandmothers |
| **subing** | Across the islands | The bamboo jaw harp (also *kubing*) — the reed-voice in the braid, kin to the bamboo flutes |

## Part VIII — Notes on the mother tongue

- The saga's Filipino vocabulary is the language of **inheritance and mercy** — the busaw, mangkukulam, babaylan, and anito bloodlines that carried the count, and the music (kulintang, agung, gangsa, kudyapi, dabakan, subing) the grandmothers braid into the metal.
- **Spanish** marks the colonial road — the friars, the galleons, the tornaviaje, the *Niño* — the historical highway the hollow rides.
- **Hebrew** names the void-before-light — Tohu, Samael, Michael — the scripture the saga re-reads.
- The count itself — "ten, eleven, twelve, and one to come" — is told in English, but it is sung in the voices of grandmothers who "decided the young must survive what the old prepared."
